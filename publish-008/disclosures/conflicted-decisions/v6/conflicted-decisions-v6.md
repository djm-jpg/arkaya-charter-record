## 1. Why this record exists

Schema Independence Charter v3 §7.6 makes a decision in which the founder's capacities conflict *"a schema-governance act published per Section 6"*. §6 requires that *"Every schema decision, version change, disciplinary-rule application and occupancy-pending null is published to the public version record."* Since 29 September 2026, every founding-period schema decision is disclosed within five business days of its effective date, and earlier where it changes something already relied on externally (D-DISCLOSURE-INTERVAL, disclosed in v4). This is the third publication run under that rule.

## 2. How to read the entries

The conventions are as v4 §2 states them, unchanged:

- **Capacity.** The capacity column names the capacity in which a decision was taken. It does not determine whether the capacities conflicted on that decision.
- **Inclusion.** An entry is included for completeness. Inclusion does not determine that the entry is a schema decision within §6, or a conflicted decision within §7.6.
- **Dates.** *Effective* is when the decision took effect. *Disclosed* is when public disclosure was completed. A later disclosure date does not mean the decision was made on that date.
- **External reliance.** Recorded on every entry. Counterparties are not named.
- **Review routes.** R-§9, R-§5.1, R-V, R-7.4 and R-P are as v3 §2 defines them. R-C is as v4 §2 corrected it: **no solicitor is instructed on these questions, and none is planned.**
- **Identifiers.** On 20 October 2026 two working sessions each issued identifiers AR-20261020-001 to -010. They are distinguished by the suffix (`-zhzg8v`, `-j7bham`), which this record always gives.

## 4. Corrections and control failures (clause 5)

**This section is not housekeeping.** It records nine places where the publication and disclosure controls did not work as stated.

1. **Release `publish-004` was published before its checks and before its production decision** (Q-3). On 20 October 2026 its deploy was uploaded one folder level too high and went to production at 14:35:50Z, before the runbook's checks (C6, C7) and production decision (C8). The public record returned 404 (not found) at its canonical addresses for about six minutes. The founder restored the previous deploy at about 14:41Z under the runbook's restoration procedure, and live verification of the previous release then passed 103 of 103 checks. The cause was the working session's deployment instruction. The release was restarted as `publish-005`.
2. **Acts taken before the step or authority the runbook places first, `publish-005`** (Q-4). The freeze was pushed to the public repository (16:02 BST) before the filing step the runbook places before it; the filing completed at 16:04 BST and the frozen bytes were those accepted. The publication patch was pushed (16:36 BST) before the typed authority naming it, which arrived at 16:37 BST and matched every value. Nothing published differs from what was approved.
3. **The same, at the production step of `publish-006`** (Q-6). GET v6 was published to production at about 22:49 BST on 20 October; the typed production approval (AR-20261020-010-j7bham) arrived at 22:50 BST and was recorded as ratifying. Every value in it matched what was published. This is the pattern of item 2, repeated.
4. **The release tag of `publish-006` was placed before the freeze** (Q-7). The runbook places the release tag on the frozen release. Tag `release-006` was placed on the pipeline code commit before any freeze, following the session's outline rather than the runbook; under the runbook's rule that tags stand, the published release carries `release-007`. In the same run, two mistaken uploads left two unpublished preview deploys in the hosting history, and the first freeze commit omitted a file and was corrected before anything was pushed. Neither affected what is public.
5. **The disclosure queue was not maintained** for the decisions of 20 October taken in the second working session (Q-5). The queue version filed that day took its rows from one session only. The decisions in rows 4 to 6 of Section 3 were recorded as "queued for disclosure" in their decision record and listed as due at a pre-publication check, but were not added to the queue until 22 October. **This is the fourth such lapse.** v4 §4 item 2 and v5 §4 item 3 record the first three.
6. **A public statement about the schema was left untrue after a later decision.** The company website's machine-readable discovery document, corrected and published at 14:40 BST on 20 October (Section 3 row 7), states that GET v6 "is prepared as a public draft but has not been published". GET v6 was published for comment at about 22:49 BST the same day. The discovery document was not updated, and on the morning of 22 October still served that statement, as did the website's `llms.txt`. No step in the publication of v6 checked other public statements about the schema. Both were corrected and published at 12:29 BST on 22 October (website change 23, merge commit `9f5bc4c`): they now state v6 as published for comment on 20 October, not approved and not a candidate, and the discovery document gives its address and SHA-256. The failure stands for the two days the statement was served.
7. **Working material for `publish-006` was held only in a session's working copy and was lost** (Q-9). On 20 October 2026 the working session held two things only in its own working copy: the release's publication log draft, and the change made by its evidence-tag step (D7). Neither was filed or committed, and that working copy no longer exists. The log draft was reproduced byte for byte on 22 October and checked against both hashes the session had reported. The evidence-tag step was re-run on 22 October under `APPROVE P-D7`, so the public record carries the time of the re-run, not of the original. The live checks of 20 and 23 October found what was public unchanged; the repository's evidence trail was incomplete from 20 to 22 October.
8. **The post-publication checks for `publish-006` ran late** (Q-10). The runbook places both the preservation and baseline step (Part E) and the next-day checks (Part F) on 21 October, the day after publication. Part E ran on 22 October. Part F ran on 23 October, after the time the founder had set for it. The live verification on 23 October passed 156 of 156 checks and found every served object byte-identical to the 20 October evidence.
9. **Release `publish-005` was not closed, and its next-day tag name was reused** (Q-11). The `publish-005` run, which published record v5 on 20 October, did not carry out its next-day checks (Part F). No next-day verification, Charter comparison or Software Heritage reading was recorded for it, and its log's next-day section still reads "Outstanding". That log reserves tag `evidence-010` for the close-out. Stop point 2 of `publish-006` issued `evidence-010` again the same evening, and it now marks the `publish-006` baseline commit. The public log of `publish-005` therefore names a tag that marks a different commit. Record v5's bytes were not affected: both verifications of `publish-006`, on 20 and 23 October, found its disclosures manifest served unchanged. Found on 23 October while closing `publish-006`. The same day the founder decided (`APPROVE PF5-1`, 16:02 BST) to close `publish-005` late under its original publication authority: a fresh check limited to what it published, a new close-out tag `evidence-012` with `evidence-010` left where it is, a dated correction to its public log in a new commit, and closing and completion notes. The close is scheduled for 27 October 2026, after this record is published, and had not been carried out at its publication.
